How it works

From a vague goal to the next thing to do.

No blank to-do list. You get a map of the ways there, and a clear pick.

1

Say where you're going

Add where you are now, your budget, your timeframe and what you can't change. That's all it needs.

2

Get a map, not a list

An AI planner (or the built-in offline one) dreams up milestones and the different routes between them, each with a real cost.

3

Follow the gold line

A* picks the cheapest route. Tick steps off, add friction where it hurts, block what's impossible, and the route updates.

The rule it was built under

The agent doesn't get to say "done".

PathProof is built with an AI coding agent. AI agents are fast, and they grade their own homework. So here, Kane CLI grades it.

  • 1The agent makes a change and tries to finish.
  • 2A hook runs Kane CLI. Kane opens Chrome and follows plain-English checks, like a person would.
  • ✕If a check fails, Kane's own account of what went wrong goes straight back to the agent. It isn't allowed to stop.
  • ✓Only when Kane passes does the work count. The report is saved in the repo.

Inside the planner

      
Small things that make it useful.

      

        
≈
Friction that learns
Mark the steps that drag. Their cost goes up, and future routes lean away from them.

        
↻
Stalled step? Split it
Milestones that sit untouched get broken into smaller, doable pieces.

        
✎
Talk to your map
Ask the built-in agent to add, explain or rework steps in plain language.

        
⇄
Many routes, one pick
Choose how many parallel routes to explore. A* still hands you one clear answer.

        
⌂
Remembers you
Your progress and friction carry across sessions and the command line.

        
◇
Works offline
No API keys? A built-in planner still draws a full map. Bring Groq, Claude or Ollama for richer ones.
